About

Research-driven. Student-centered.

I am a Ph.D. candidate in Mathematics with a concentration in Statistics at Texas Tech University. My research develops statistically rigorous and geometrically motivated methods for comparing covariance structures and for understanding classical inference through new geometric perspectives.

My work combines multivariate statistics, nonparametric inference, matrix analysis, and the geometry of symmetric positive definite matrices. I am also deeply committed to teaching and to creating learning environments in which students build confidence, mathematical reasoning, and practical problem-solving skills.

Research

Current research directions

Developing interpretable statistical procedures with strong theoretical foundations.

01

Covariance Matrix Equality Testing

I develop nonparametric and geometric procedures for testing equality of covariance matrices, with particular attention to settings where classical likelihood-based assumptions are restrictive.

02

Geometry of SPD Matrices

My dissertation investigates Euclidean and log-Euclidean structures on the manifold of symmetric positive definite matrices, including metric-dependent Fréchet means and geometric signatures of covariance inequality.

03

Arithmetic–Geometric Mean Inference

I study how discrepancies between arithmetic and geometric pooled means can reveal structure in two-sample inference, including second-order asymptotics and chi-square-based procedures.

04

High-Dimensional Extensions

Future directions include regularization and shrinkage for high-dimensional covariance problems, extensions to multiple populations, and inference for manifold-valued data.
